Master's Engineering Project

Vertical-Axis Wind Turbine Aeroacoustics

Computational investigation of the aerodynamic noise generated by a vertical-axis wind turbine using unsteady CFD, aeroacoustic analysis and signal processing.

Project Overview

This project investigated the aerodynamic noise produced by a vertical-axis wind turbine (VAWT), with particular focus on identifying and characterising tonal noise associated with the rotating turbine.

A two-dimensional computational model was developed in ANSYS Fluent to simulate the unsteady aerodynamic flow around the turbine. Acoustic pressure data were then analysed to examine the frequency content and directional characteristics of the generated noise.

Objectives

  • Develop an unsteady CFD model of a vertical-axis wind turbine.
  • Resolve the transient aerodynamic behaviour of the rotating blades.
  • investigate the acoustic pressure field surrounding the turbine.
  • Use frequency-domain analysis to identify dominant tonal components of the generated noise.
  • Assess how the radiated noise varies with direction around the turbine.

Computational Method

The turbine was represented using a two-dimensional transient CFD model. The computational domain incorporated a rotating region around the turbine and a stationary surrounding domain.

The analysis used the Transition SST turbulence model together with Scale-Adaptive / Scale-Resolving methods to capture transient flow structures relevant to aerodynamic noise generation.

Rotor Diameter

1.03 m

Freestream Velocity

9 m/s

Tip-Speed Ratio

7

Acoustic Receivers

24

Twenty-four virtual acoustic receivers were positioned at 15° intervals around the turbine at a radius of three rotor diameters from the axis of rotation.

Aeroacoustic Analysis

Transient pressure signals obtained from the numerical model were processed to investigate the acoustic response of the turbine.

Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) analysis was used to convert pressure-time histories into the frequency domain, allowing tonal components associated with the turbine's rotational behaviour and blade-passing frequency to be identified.

Receiver data distributed around the turbine were also used to investigate the directivity of the radiated acoustic field.
